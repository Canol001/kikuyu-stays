# Kikuyu Stays

Proposed business concept

A local accommodation booking platform for Kikuyu and its surrounding areas.\
Customers visit the website, discover available houses, view photographs, compare prices, contact hosts or book accommodation.

The platform could accommodate:

- Airbnb-style furnished apartments.
- Bedsitters and studios.
- One-bedroom and two-bedroom apartments.
- Guest houses.
- Holiday homes.
- Short-stay accommodation.
- Serviced apartments.
- Houses for weekly and monthly stays.

The key selling point would be local knowledge, verified properties and a central place to discover accommodation.

Potential customers:

- People visiting Kikuyu.
- Students and visiting lecturers.
- People attending weddings, funerals and other events.
- Business travellers.
- People relocating to Kikuyu.
- People looking for temporary accommodation.

# 2. How the platform should work

I would design it around three types of users.

1\. Customers / Guests

People looking for accommodation.

- Browse available houses.
- Filter by location, price, bedrooms and amenities.
- View photographs and house details.
- Check availability.
- Make booking requests.
- Pay through M-Pesa.
- Receive booking confirmations.
- Rate and review their stays.

  2\. Property owners / Hosts

  People who own or manage houses.
- Register an account.
- Submit their properties.
- Upload house photographs.
- Set prices per night or month.
- Set availability.
- Receive booking requests.
- Accept or reject requests.
- View earnings and booking history.

  3\. Platform owner / Administrator

  The person controlling the entire marketplace.
- Approve or reject property listings.
- Verify property owners.
- Manage customers and hosts.
- Monitor bookings.
- Configure platform commissions.
- Monitor payments.
- Suspend fraudulent accounts.
- Manage disputes and complaints.
- View revenue and business reports.

The third role is particularly important. The platform owner must have complete administrative control without needing to contact a developer every time they want to change something.

# 3. The most important decision: how the owner makes money

There are several ways to operate this business. I would not rely on advertisements alone.

Here are four possible revenue models.

## Model A: Commission per successful booking

The platform takes a percentage of every booking.

For example:

- Guest books a house for KES 3,000 per night.
- Guest stays for 3 nights.
- Total booking = KES 9,000.
- Platform commission = 10%.
- Platform earns KES 900.
- Property owner receives KES 8,100, before any separately agreed payment charges.

Example booking revenue

Host: 90%

## Model B: Monthly subscription for property owners

Hosts pay a fixed amount to advertise their properties.

Illustrative pricing:

| Plan         | Price           | Features           |
| ------------ | --------------- | ------------------ |
| Free         | KES 0           | 1 property listing |
| Basic        | KES 500/month   | 3 listings         |
| Professional | KES 1,000/month | 10 listings        |
| Business     | KES 2,500/month | 30 listings        |

The advantage is predictable revenue even when bookings are low.

The disadvantage is that new hosts may resist paying before they have received customers.

## Model C: Featured property listings

Property owners pay to have their properties promoted.

For example:

- Featured listing: KES 200 for 7 days.
- Premium listing: KES 500 for 30 days.
- Homepage promotion: KES 1,000 for 30 days.

The platform can display sponsored properties separately from normal search results.

## Model D: Hybrid model

My recommendation

Combine the four models, but introduce them gradually.

| Revenue source     | Launch strategy               |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Booking commission | Main revenue source           |
| Host subscription  | Introduce later               |
| Featured listings  | Optional from launch          |
| Advertising        | Introduce after traffic grows |

I would initially charge hosts 0% subscription and take a 8% to 10% commission on completed bookings. This makes onboarding easier.

# 6. Booking and payment architecture

For a Kenyan marketplace, M-Pesa should be a first-class payment option.

I would build the following booking process:

1. Guest selects property

   Chooses check-in date, checkout date and number of guests.
2. System checks availability

   Prevents overlapping confirmed bookings.
3. Guest submits booking

   The booking enters pending status.
4. Host accepts

   Or the platform confirms automatically if instant booking is enabled.
5. Guest pays

   M-Pesa STK Push or another supported payment method.
6. Payment callback arrives

   Backend validates the transaction and updates booking status.
7. Guest receives confirmation

   Booking reference, property address and check-in instructions.
8. Host receives payout

   According to the agreed settlement and cancellation policy.
9. Guest completes stay

   Can leave a review after checkout.

### How should M-Pesa money flow?

There are two approaches:

Option 1: Platform collects the full payment.

Guest pays the platform. The platform deducts its commission and settles the host.

For example:

- Guest pays KES 10,000.
- Platform commission is KES 1,000.
- Host receives KES 9,000.

This gives the owner more control over payments, but creates settlement, reconciliation and regulatory responsibilities. You should agree the appropriate Safaricom Daraja payment structure with the provider before implementing this.

Option 2: Host receives payment directly.

The platform records the booking and charges the host separately for its commission or subscription.

This is easier to start with, but it makes it harder to guarantee that the platform receives its commission.

My preference: eventually implement controlled marketplace payments, but begin with a simpler, properly documented payment arrangement while validating demand.

One critical technical detail: implement database-level protection against overlapping confirmed reservations. Otherwise, two people could book the same house for the same dates.

Also, every payment must have a unique transaction reference, and M-Pesa callbacks must be idempotent. Never trust a frontend success message as proof of payment.

# 10. Trust, security and Kenyan regulations

This is not an area to cut corners.

The platform is connecting strangers to private residential properties. Trust is part of the product itself.

I would require:

# 14. The business ownership arrangement

| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Domain name                   | Business owner                                                 |
| Hosting account               | Business owner                                                 |
| Source code ownership         | As agreed in development contract                              |

The owner should have a master administrator account. You, as the developer, can have a separate technical maintenance account with appropriately limited permissions.

That avoids a situation where the business owner has to call the developer every time they want to approve a house or change a commission rate.

Short stays only (Airbnb-style, per night).

Long-term rentals (monthly).

Both short-term and long-term rentals.

3\. Who should control the bookings?

Property owners accept or reject bookings.

Platform administrator controls all bookings.
